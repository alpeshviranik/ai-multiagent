# Diabetes Basics

## Types of diabetes
Type 1 diabetes occurs when the body does not make insulin and requires insulin treatment. Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body does not use insulin well and is often managed with lifestyle changes, medicines, and sometimes insulin. Gestational diabetes develops during pregnancy.

## Monitoring blood sugar
Many adults aim for a fasting range of about 80 to 130 mg/dL, but personal targets vary. Your care team will set targets for you. An A1C test shows your average blood sugar over about 3 months.

## Healthy eating
Spread carbohydrates through the day, choose whole grains, vegetables, and lean proteins, and limit sugary drinks. A registered dietitian can build a plan that fits your culture and preferences.

## Physical activity
Regular activity, such as 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week, helps lower blood sugar. Check your sugar before and after exercise if you take insulin or certain pills, and carry fast-acting carbs.

## Foot care
Check your feet daily for cuts, blisters, redness, or swelling. Wash and dry them gently, wear well-fitting shoes, and see a clinician promptly for any sore that does not heal.
